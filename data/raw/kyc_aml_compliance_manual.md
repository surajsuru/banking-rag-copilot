# KYC and AML Compliance Manual

## Version 2.4 | Effective Date: 01 April 2025

## 1. Introduction and Regulatory Framework

This manual establishes the Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) compliance requirements for the banking platform. Compliance is mandatory under:
- Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA) 2002
- RBI Master Direction on KYC (Updated 2023)
- FATF Recommendations
- Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) 1999

## 2. Customer Risk Classification

### 2.1 Risk Categories
Every customer must be assigned a risk rating at onboarding and reviewed periodically:

LOW RISK customers:
- Salaried individuals with regular income source
- Government employees
- Self-employed in established professions
- Small businesses with clear business purpose
- Review frequency: Every 10 years

MEDIUM RISK customers:
- Self-employed individuals with variable income
- Small traders and merchants
- Non-resident Indians
- Foreign nationals
- Businesses with complex ownership
- Review frequency: Every 5 years

HIGH RISK customers:
- Politically Exposed Persons (PEP) and their family members
- Non-face-to-face customers (video KYC / digital onboarding)
- Customers from high-risk jurisdictions
- Cash-intensive businesses (petrol stations, jewelers, money changers)
- Shell companies or complex corporate structures
- Prior AML flags or STR filings
- Review frequency: Every 2 years or triggered

PROHIBITED customers (cannot be onboarded):
- Individuals on OFAC SDN list
- UN Security Council designated entities
- Individuals convicted of financial crimes (within 7 years)
- Shell banks
- Anonymous accounts

## 3. Customer Identification Requirements

### 3.1 Individual Customers - Proof of Identity (any one)
- Aadhaar Card (UIDAI-verified)
- Passport
- Voter Identity Card
- Driving License
- PAN Card (mandatory for transactions above INR 50,000)
- NREGA Job Card

### 3.2 Individual Customers - Proof of Address (any one)
- Aadhaar Card
- Passport
- Voter Identity Card
- Driving License
- Utility bill (not older than 3 months)
- Bank statement (not older than 3 months)
- Employer letter

### 3.3 Corporate Customers - Required Documents
- Certificate of Incorporation
- Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Board Resolution authorizing account opening
- List of authorized signatories with specimen signatures
- Beneficial Ownership declaration (ownership above 25%)
- PAN of company and all beneficial owners
- Proof of registered office address

### 3.4 Beneficial Ownership Threshold
Any individual owning more than 25% of a corporate entity must provide KYC documents. For trusts, all trustees and beneficiaries with 25%+ beneficial interest must complete KYC.

## 4. eKYC and Video KYC

### 4.1 Aadhaar-based eKYC
Permitted for resident Indian individuals. Requires customer consent and UIDAI authentication. eKYC customers are onboarded as low-risk unless other factors indicate higher risk.

### 4.2 Video KYC Process
Step 1: Customer initiates video KYC request through mobile app
Step 2: Bank officer schedules video call (within 48 hours)
Step 3: Officer verifies customer face against Aadhaar photo
Step 4: Customer displays original ID documents to camera
Step 5: Officer captures screenshots of documents
Step 6: Customer provides live consent on video
Step 7: Officer completes KYC form and submits for approval
Step 8: KYC approved within 24 hours

Video KYC requirements:
- Customer must be in India during the call
- Video recording must be stored for 5 years
- Poor video quality: reschedule, do not approve
- Officer must not share screen or customer data during call

## 5. Periodic KYC Review

### 5.1 Triggered Reviews
A KYC review must be triggered immediately when:
- Customer risk rating changes
- Adverse media found on customer
- Customer added to watchlist
- Large unusual transaction detected
- Customer reports change of address or occupation
- Account dormancy followed by large transaction

### 5.2 KYC Expiry and Account Restrictions
When KYC is about to expire:
- 90 days before expiry: Send first notice to customer
- 60 days before expiry: Send second notice
- 30 days before expiry: Restrict outward remittances
- On expiry: Restrict all debits; allow credits only
- 30 days after expiry: Full account freeze
- 180 days after expiry: Account closure proceedings

## 6. Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

### 6.1 When EDD is Required
- All high-risk customers
- All PEP customers and their family members
- Customers from high-risk jurisdictions
- Non-face-to-face customers
- Complex or unusual business structures
- When standard due diligence cannot be completed

### 6.2 EDD Measures
- Senior management approval for account opening
- Identify source of funds with supporting documentation
- Identify source of wealth
- Enhanced ongoing monitoring
- More frequent KYC reviews (annually)

## 7. PEP (Politically Exposed Persons) Policy

### 7.1 PEP Definition
Individuals entrusted with prominent public functions:
- Heads of state or government
- Senior government officials
- Senior judicial officials
- Senior military officials
- Senior executives of state-owned corporations
- Important political party officials
- Family members and close associates of PEPs

### 7.2 PEP Onboarding Process
1. Identify customer as PEP through screening
2. Obtain Board-level or Senior Management approval
3. Complete Enhanced Due Diligence
4. Obtain source of funds and source of wealth documentation
5. Apply enhanced monitoring permanently
6. Annual review with senior management sign-off

## 8. Sanctions Screening

### 8.1 Screening Lists
All customers and counterparties are screened against:
- OFAC SDN (Specially Designated Nationals) list
- UN Security Council Sanctions list
- EU Consolidated Sanctions list
- GOI (Government of India) Proscribed List
- Interpol Red Notice list
- Internal watchlist

### 8.2 Screening Frequency
- New customers: At onboarding
- Existing customers: Daily automated screening
- Transaction counterparties: Real-time at transaction initiation
- Beneficial owners: At onboarding and at each KYC review

### 8.3 Hit Resolution Process
True Match:
1. Do not open account / block transaction immediately
2. Alert Compliance team within 30 minutes
3. Compliance notifies Senior Management within 1 hour
4. File regulatory report within 24 hours
5. Document all steps taken

False Positive:
1. Document the screening hit and why it is a false positive
2. Two-person review and sign-off required
3. Update system to reduce future false positives
4. Retain documentation for 5 years

## 9. Record Keeping Requirements

All KYC and AML records must be maintained as follows:
- Account opening documents: For lifetime of account plus 10 years after closure
- Transaction records: 10 years from transaction date
- STR filings: 10 years from filing date
- Training records: 5 years
- Risk assessments: 5 years from date of assessment

Records must be readily available to regulators within 48 hours of request. Electronic records must be encrypted and backed up daily.

## 10. AML Training Requirements

All relevant staff must complete:
- Initial AML training: Before handling any customer accounts (minimum 4 hours)
- Annual refresher training: Every 12 months (minimum 2 hours)
- Specialized training for AML team members: Quarterly (minimum 2 hours)
- New typology briefings: As issued by Compliance team

Training completion must be documented. Non-compliance with training requirements is a disciplinary matter.
